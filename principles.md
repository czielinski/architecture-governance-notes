# Principles

Six principles I keep coming back to when governance is supposed to help rather than slow things down.

**1. Governance produces decisions, not process.** The product of a governance function is a set of reliable answers people can build on. Measure it by questions decided and reused, not by meetings held or documents produced.

**2. Guardrails before gates.** A gate is an approval event: it scales with request volume and consumes senior attention case by case. A guardrail is a decision made once for a whole class of cases: it scales with reuse. Whenever the same request pattern shows up for the third time, that is a guardrail waiting to be written.

**3. One owner per question.** Every recurring question needs exactly one accountable owner. Shared ownership is the polite form of no ownership, and gray areas are where complexity breeds: they generate escalations, workarounds, and shadow decisions.

**4. Decide once, write it down.** An undocumented decision is a scheduled repeat discussion. The half-life of a verbal decision is one reorganization. Documentation is not bureaucracy; it is the mechanism that lets a decision be reused instead of re-litigated.

**5. Smallest sufficient process.** Every step in a governance process must remove a question for the people going through it. A step that adds questions, or exists only to inform someone who never acts on the information, is friction wearing a governance costume.

**6. Watch decision latency.** The honest KPI of governance is the time from "question raised" to "answer usable". If that number grows, the function is failing, whatever the compliance statistics say.
